# v0.4.0 Calendar — design specification

**Status:** Released 2026-09-18. Migration `0007` is applied and isolated live
database acceptance passes. Physical-iPhone and keyboard/focus/contrast checks
remain as post-release follow-up.

## Release outcome

Give every Member a shared Calendar for the Household while completing the related
timezone, Membership exit, ownership transfer, and To-do assignment foundations.

## Confirmed decisions

### Permissions and Events

- Every Member may create, edit, and delete every Event.
- Events may be all-day or timed, single-day or multi-day.
- An Event requires a 1–100 character title and start. Its end is optional and, if
  present, must be after the start.
- An all-day Event without an end occupies one day. An all-day end date is inclusive.
- A timed Event without an end displays only its start time.
- Event details are optional plain text limited to 2,000 characters.
- Past Events remain available indefinitely.
- Deleting an Event requires confirmation.
- Hearth records the creating Member and creation/update timestamps. The Event
  details screen shows who added it; the month grid does not emphasize authorship.
- If that Member leaves, the Event remains and identifies its creator as
  "Former Member" rather than retaining a copied profile snapshot.
- Two Members may edit the same Event, but Hearth detects a stale save instead of
  silently overwriting newer changes. The stale form remains intact for review.
- External calendar synchronization is excluded.

### Calendar presentation

- Desktop opens to a month view with a nearby upcoming-events list.
- Phones open to the upcoming list with an easy switch to month view.
- Phone month view shows compact truncated time-and-title entries and an overflow
  count rather than reducing Events to unlabeled dots.
- A clear Add Event action is always available. Selecting a date prefills that date.
- Selecting an Event opens a responsive details screen with Edit and Delete actions.
- Multi-day Events follow the supplied Google Calendar reference: a continuous bar
  spans the affected date cells and continues across week boundaries as needed.
- Single-day timed Events appear as compact time-and-title rows in month cells.
- A date with more Events than fit shows an explicit overflow count.
- Month cells show up to four Events on desktop and three on phones before the
  overflow count, reducing this only when available screen height requires it.
- The upcoming list shows a multi-day Event once with its complete range.
- The upcoming list covers the next seven days. If that window is empty, it shows
  the next future Event.
- Weeks begin on Sunday. Adjacent-month dates remain visible in a muted style.
- Month navigation provides Today and previous/next controls.
- Overlapping Events are valid and display without warnings or restrictions.
- All-day and multi-day Events appear before timed Events; timed Events sort by
  start time. An Event appears in every date and upcoming window it overlaps.
- Calendar is the first feature card on the home hub.
- Until configurable Event colors arrive, Events use one soft blue treatment;
  navigation uses green accents and today uses restrained yellow highlighting.
- The seven-day time-grid view shown in the supplied reference is deferred to
  v0.4.1 so v0.4.0 can focus on month and upcoming views.

### Household timezone

- The Household owns one timezone.
- Existing Households begin with `America/Chicago`.
- New Households begin with the creator's browser-detected timezone.
- Only an owner may change it from Household people.
- Owners choose from a friendly searchable list backed by IANA timezone identifiers.
- Event forms clearly identify and always use the Household timezone, regardless of
  a Member's current device timezone.
- Event times display in the Household timezone.
- Changing the Household timezone preserves each Event's absolute moment; displayed
  clock times change to reflect the newly selected timezone.
- All-day Events remain on their selected calendar dates when the timezone changes.
- Before saving a timezone change, Hearth identifies the old and new zones and
  explains those timed and all-day effects.
- Nonexistent local times during a daylight-saving jump are rejected with an
  explanation. Repeated local times during a rollback are presented as two
  timezone-labeled choices rather than guessed.
- Chore scheduling remains on its current behavior in v0.4.0. Household-timezone
  Chores and Calendar-visible Chores are planned for v0.4.1.

### Membership exit and ownership

- A non-owner Member may leave after explicit confirmation.
- Leaving rotates the Invite Code, preserves the departing User's User Profile and
  Events, and makes their assigned Chores and To-dos Unassigned.
- An owner transfers ownership to another Member as a separate confirmed action.
- Each Household has exactly one owner at all times.
- After transfer, the prior owner becomes a regular Member and may leave separately.
- A sole owner cannot leave or delete the Household in this release.
- Ownership transfer and voluntary leave appear in a clearly labeled Household
  management area on Household people.
- Transfer does not rotate the Invite Code. Leaving does.

### To-do Assignees

- A To-do has zero or one Assignee.
- Members may select an Assignee during creation or reassign immediately afterward.
- The interface shows the Assignee's Avatar and display name.
- Unassigned remains available.
- A completed To-do retains and displays its Assignee until normal cleanup removes
  the To-do. A departing Member is unassigned from active and completed To-dos.

### Form and navigation behavior

- Event forms suggest 15-minute increments but accept exact-minute times.
- Switching a timed Event to all-day hides but temporarily remembers its times;
  saving it as all-day clears them. Switching an existing all-day Event to timed
  starts at 9:00 AM with no end.
- Failed creates or edits preserve every entered value and show an inline error.
- The selected month is represented in the URL so refresh, back, and forward retain
  Calendar position.
- Event details and editing use stable routes rather than dialog-only state.
- An empty Calendar invites the Member to add the first Event.
- A deleted or inaccessible Event route explains that it is unavailable and links
  back to Calendar.

## Durable data model

- A Household stores one IANA Household timezone.
- A timed Event stores absolute start and optional end moments.
- An all-day Event stores start and optional inclusive end calendar dates.
- Database constraints prevent mixed or invalid timed/all-day states and require an
  end, when present, to follow the start.
- Every Event belongs to one Household. Current Members may read and modify its
  Events through RLS; other Households cannot.
- The creator relationship is nullable so an Event survives Membership deletion
  and becomes attributable to a Former Member.
- Optimistic concurrency uses the Event's update version or timestamp to reject a
  stale edit.
- Each Household has exactly one owner. Narrow transactional database functions
  perform ownership transfer and voluntary leave without exposing direct role or
  Membership mutation to clients.
- A To-do's optional Assignee references a Membership and becomes Unassigned when
  that Membership is deleted.

## Required acceptance checks

- Create, edit, delete, and validate timed and all-day Events.
- Render single-day and multi-day Events across month and year boundaries.
- Validate daylight-saving gaps and repeated times.
- Display and change the Household timezone without shifting all-day dates.
- Detect concurrent Event edits without discarding the stale form.
- Prove RLS isolation between Households.
- Transfer ownership and voluntarily leave while maintaining exactly one owner.
- Assign, reassign, complete, and automatically unassign To-dos.
- Verify month and upcoming views at desktop and iPhone widths.
- Verify keyboard navigation, visible focus, and readable contrast.

## Known implementation facts

- The next ordered database migration is `0007`.
- Hearth has no existing Event table or Calendar route.
- Households do not currently store a timezone.
- Chore calculations and completed To-do cleanup currently assume
  `America/Chicago`.
- To-dos do not currently store an Assignee; Chores already establish the nullable
  Membership foreign-key and automatic-Unassigned precedent.
- Membership exit and ownership transfer require narrow transactional database
  functions so authorization and multi-row changes remain atomic under RLS.
